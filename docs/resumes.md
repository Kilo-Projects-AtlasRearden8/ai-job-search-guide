# Resumes — Drafting and Tailoring with AI

## What a resume needs

A resume is a one-page (or two-page, if you have lots of experience) summary of your work history. Most employers spend only a few seconds on the first look, so it should be easy to scan.

A basic resume includes:

- Your name and contact information (email and phone — see the Privacy chapter before putting your address online)
- A short summary (2–3 sentences about who you are and what you want)
- Experience (jobs you have held, with dates and what you did)
- Education (schools, degrees, or certifications)
- Skills (tools, languages, or abilities relevant to the job)
- Licenses and certifications (especially important in healthcare)

## Step 1: Gather your facts

Before you ask AI for help, write down:

- Where you worked, your job title, and the dates
- What you did each day (3–5 bullet points per job)
- Any numbers that show your impact (for example: "cared for 6–8 patients per shift")
- Your education and any licenses or certifications

## Step 2: Ask AI to draft a resume

Copy this prompt and fill in the brackets:

> You are a friendly career coach. Write a one-page resume in plain English. Use these details:
> - Name: [Your Name]
> - Email: [Your Email]
> - Phone: [Your Phone]
> - Summary: [2–3 sentences about you]
> - Experience: [List your jobs, dates, and main duties]
> - Education: [Your school, degree, or certification]
> - Skills: [Your skills]
> - Licenses/certifications: [Any licenses]
>
> Use sections: Summary, Experience, Education, Skills, Licenses. Use bullet points. Keep each bullet to one line. Do not invent jobs, dates, or skills. Use placeholders like [City] and [State] instead of a real home address.

## Step 3: Tailor the resume to a job posting

Employers like to see that you read the job posting. Copy the job description into the AI and ask it to match your resume to it:

> Here is a job posting: [paste the job description]
>
> Here is my current resume: [paste your resume draft]
>
> Rewrite my resume to highlight the skills and experience this job asks for. Keep it to one page. Use plain English. Do not invent experience I do not have.

## Step 4: Read it out loud

Read the resume out loud. If a sentence sounds stiff or unnatural, change it. Your resume should sound like you, just a polished version.

## Step 5: Save multiple versions

Save one master resume with everything, and separate versions for each type of job you apply to (for example: one for hospital jobs, one for clinic jobs). Name the files clearly so you do not send the wrong one:

- Resume_Master.docx
- Resume_Hospital_ChargeNurse.docx
- Resume_Clinic_Triage.docx

## What never to put on a resume you paste online

See the Privacy & Safety chapter. Never paste your full home address, Social Security number, date of birth, or bank information into an AI tool or post it on a job board.
