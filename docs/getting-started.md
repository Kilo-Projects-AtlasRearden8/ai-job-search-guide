# Getting Started — Talking to AI

## What AI is, in plain English

AI is a computer program that has read a huge amount of writing and learned the patterns of how people use words. When you type something to it, it guesses the most helpful words to come next. It is not a person, it does not have feelings, and it does not know you. It is more like a very well-read assistant that works instantly and never gets tired.

You do not need to be technical to use AI. If you can type a question into a search box, you can use AI.

## Where to use AI

You can use AI in a web browser on your phone or computer. Popular free options include tools from Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, and Meta. Most of them let you start a new "chat" and type your question. Pick one and stick with it while you learn.

None of the steps in this guide require you to pay for anything. Free AI tools are enough to write resumes, cover letters, and practice interviews.

## How to write a good prompt

A prompt is just what you type to the AI. The better your prompt, the better the answer. Use this simple recipe:

1. **Role** — tell the AI who to be (for example: "You are a friendly career coach.")
2. **Task** — tell it exactly what to do (for example: "Help me write a resume.")
3. **Details** — give it the facts it needs (your job history, the job you want, your tone).
4. **Format** — tell it how you want the answer back (for example: "Give me bullet points." or "Write it as a short email.")

### Example: a weak prompt
> Write me a resume.

This works, but the result will be generic.

### Example: a strong prompt
> You are a friendly career coach. I am a registered nurse with three years of medical-surgical experience. I want to apply for a charge nurse position at a local hospital. Write a one-page resume with sections for summary, experience, education, and licenses. Use bullet points and plain English. Do not include my real name, address, or phone number — use placeholders like [Your Name].

## Start small

You do not have to do everything at once. Pick one task from this guide — for example, rewriting one bullet point on your resume — and try it. Once that feels easy, move to the next chapter.

## A reminder before we begin

AI can make mistakes. It might invent dates, job titles, or skills you do not have. Always read what the AI writes and check that it is true before you send it to an employer. AI is a helper; you are still in charge.
