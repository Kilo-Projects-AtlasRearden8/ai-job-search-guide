# Privacy & Safety — What Never to Paste Online

## The most important rule

Never paste anything into an AI chat, job board, or public form that you would not want a stranger to see. Assume that anything you type could be saved, read by others, or used to train future AI.

## Never paste these

- Your full home address
- Your Social Security number
- Your date of birth (full)
- Your bank account or credit card numbers
- Your driver's license or passport number
- Your current employer's confidential information
- Patient names or medical details (especially important if you work in healthcare)
- Passwords or security answers

## Safe alternatives

Instead of your real details, use placeholders:

- [Your Name]
- [City, State] (not your full street address)
- [Your Email] (use an email you do not mind sharing)
- [Your Phone]
- [Employer Name]
- [Date]

When AI asks for details to tailor your resume, give general facts ("three years of nursing experience") rather than specific patient stories or facility names.

## Job boards and public profiles

Job boards like LinkedIn, Indeed, and others are public or semi-public. Do not post your full address or date of birth there. Use a city and state at most. Be careful about sharing where you work and when you are off — that information can be misused.

## Healthcare workers: extra care

If you are a nurse or other healthcare worker, never share patient names, room numbers, diagnoses, or anything that could identify a patient — not even to "remove the name." Small details added together can still identify someone. This is both a privacy rule and, in many places, a legal requirement (such as HIPAA in the United States).

AI is for education and writing help. It is not a place to discuss real patient cases.

## If you accidentally paste something sensitive

- Delete the chat if the tool allows it.
- Change any passwords that were exposed.
- If you shared work or patient information, tell your supervisor or compliance officer right away.
