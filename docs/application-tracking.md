# Application Tracking — Keeping Track of Where You Applied

## Why track your applications

When you apply to several jobs, it is easy to forget which ones you applied to, when, and who to follow up with. A simple tracker keeps you organized and reduces stress.

You can track applications in a notebook, a spreadsheet, or the browser-based tracker included in this guide (`site/tracker.html`). The tracker saves only in your browser — your data never leaves your computer.

## What to track for each job

- Company name
- Job title
- Date you applied
- How you applied (website, email, in person)
- Contact person (if you have one)
- Follow-up date (about one week after applying)
- Status (Applied, Interviewing, Offer, Declined, No response)
- Notes (anything useful to remember)

## Step 1: Start a list

Open `site/tracker.html` in your browser, or draw a table in a notebook. Add one row per job application.

## Step 2: Update it weekly

Pick one day each week — Sunday morning works well — and update your tracker. Mark which applications have not heard back, and send a polite follow-up email if it has been about a week.

## Step 3: Follow up politely

Here is a simple follow-up email you can ask AI to write:

> Write a short, polite follow-up email for a job application. I applied for a [Job Title] at [Company] on [Date]. I have not heard back yet. Keep it friendly and under 100 words. Use plain English.

## Step 4: Celebrate small wins

Applying to jobs is hard work. Every application you submit is a win. Track them so you can see your progress.
