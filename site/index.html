1. Getting Started — Talking to AI

AI is a computer program that has read a huge amount of writing and learned how people use words. When you type something to it, it guesses the most helpful words to come next. It is not a person — it is more like a well-read assistant that works instantly.

You do not need to be technical. If you can type into a search box, you can use AI. Free tools from Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, and Meta are enough for everything in this guide.

How to write a good prompt

  1. Role — tell the AI who to be (for example: "You are a friendly career coach.")
  2. Task — tell it exactly what to do (for example: "Help me write a resume.")
  3. Details — give it the facts it needs (your job history, the job you want, your tone).
  4. Format — tell it how you want the answer back (for example: "Use bullet points.")

Weak prompt: "Write me a resume."

Better prompt: "You are a friendly career coach. I am a registered nurse with three years of medical-surgical experience. I want to apply for a charge nurse position. Write a one-page resume with sections for summary, experience, education, and licenses. Use bullet points and plain English. Use placeholders like [Your Name] instead of my real name."

2. Resumes — Drafting and Tailoring with AI

A resume is a one-page summary of your work history. It should be easy to scan. Include: contact information, a short summary, experience, education, skills, and licenses.

Step 1: Gather your facts

Write down where you worked, your job title, the dates, what you did each day, any numbers showing your impact, and your education and licenses.

Step 2: Ask AI to draft a resume

You are a friendly career coach. Write a one-page resume in plain English. Use these details:
- Name: [Your Name]
- Email: [Your Email]
- Phone: [Your Phone]
- Summary: [2-3 sentences about you]
- Experience: [jobs, dates, main duties]
- Education: [school, degree, certification]
- Skills: [your skills]
- Licenses/certifications: [any licenses]

Use sections: Summary, Experience, Education, Skills, Licenses. Bullet points, one line each. Do not invent jobs, dates, or skills. Use [City, State] instead of a real address.

Step 3: Tailor the resume to a job posting

Paste the job description and your draft into AI:

Here is a job posting: [paste the job description]
Here is my current resume: [paste your resume draft]
Rewrite my resume to highlight the skills this job asks for. Keep it to one page. Plain English. Do not invent experience I do not have.

Step 4: Read it out loud

If a sentence sounds stiff, change it. Your resume should sound like you, just polished.

Step 5: Save multiple versions

Save one master resume and separate versions per job type. Name them clearly so you do not send the wrong one:

  • Resume_Master.docx
  • Resume_Hospital_ChargeNurse.docx
  • Resume_Clinic_Triage.docx

3. Cover Letters — Writing Letters That Sound Like You

A cover letter is a short letter that goes with your resume. It explains why you want the job and why you are a good fit. Keep it to one page: opening, body, story, closing.

Step 1: Gather what you need

Job title, company name, hiring manager's name (or "Dear Hiring Manager"), 2–3 things from the job posting that match your experience, and a short work story.

Step 2: Ask AI to draft a cover letter

You are a friendly writing coach. Write a one-page cover letter in plain English, warm but professional.
Job: [Job Title] at [Company]
Hiring manager: [Name or "Dear Hiring Manager"]
My experience: [2-3 sentences about your background]
Why I want this job: [1-2 sentences]
A story about my work: [short example, 2-3 sentences]

Structure: opening, body paragraph connecting experience to the job, short story paragraph, closing asking for an interview. Plain English. Do not sound robotic. Use [City, State] for address.

Step 3: Make it sound like you

Read it out loud. Rewrite anything you would not actually say. Use AI for structure, then make the words your own.

Step 4: Check the details

  • Company name spelled correctly?
  • Right job title?
  • Email and phone correct?
  • No leftover placeholders like [Company]?

4. Interview Practice — Practicing with AI

AI is a safe place to practice because it does not judge you and never gets tired. It helps you feel more confident, but it is not a substitute for a real interview.

Step 1: Ask AI to be the interviewer

You are a hiring manager interviewing for a [Job Title] role at a [type of company]. Ask me one interview question at a time. Wait for my answer, then give kind, honest feedback. Then ask the next question. Start with "Tell me about yourself."

Step 2: Answer out loud

Answer each question out loud, then read the AI's feedback. Try again if it suggests a better way to say something.

Step 3: Practice common questions

Give me 10 common interview questions for a [Job Title] role, with tips on how to answer each one in plain English.

Common questions: Tell me about yourself. Why do you want this job? What is your greatest strength? What challenge did you face at work, and how did you handle it? Why should we hire you?

Step 4: Practice your own questions

I am interviewing for a [Job Title] role. Give me 3 good questions to ask the employer at the end of the interview.

5. Application Tracking — Keeping Track of Where You Applied

When you apply to several jobs, a simple tracker keeps you organized. You can use a notebook, a spreadsheet, or the browser-based tracker in this guide (open tracker). The tracker saves only in your browser — your data never leaves your computer.

What to track for each job

  • Company name
  • Job title
  • Date you applied
  • How you applied (website, email, in person)
  • Contact person (if you have one)
  • Follow-up date (about one week after applying)
  • Status (Applied, Interviewing, Offer, Declined, No response)
  • Notes

Step 1: Start a list

Open site/tracker.html in your browser, or draw a table in a notebook.

Step 2: Update it weekly

Pick one day each week to update your tracker and send polite follow-up emails if you have not heard back.

Step 3: Follow up politely

Write a short, polite follow-up email for a job application. I applied for a [Job Title] at [Company] on [Date]. I have not heard back yet. Keep it friendly and under 100 words. Plain English.

6. Privacy & Safety — What Never to Paste Online

Never paste anything into an AI chat, job board, or public form that you would not want a stranger to see. Assume anything you type could be saved or read by others.

Never paste these

  • Your full home address
  • Your Social Security number
  • Your full date of birth
  • Bank account or credit card numbers
  • Driver's license or passport numbers
  • Your current employer's confidential information
  • Patient names or medical details
  • Passwords or security answers

Safe alternatives

Use placeholders: [Your Name], [City, State], [Your Email], [Your Phone], [Employer Name], [Date].

Healthcare workers: extra care

If you are a nurse or healthcare worker, never share patient names, room numbers, diagnoses, or anything that could identify a patient — not even "with the name removed." AI is for education and writing help, not for discussing real patient cases.

If you accidentally paste something sensitive

  • Delete the chat if the tool allows it.
  • Change any exposed passwords.
  • If you shared work or patient information, tell your supervisor or compliance officer right away.
